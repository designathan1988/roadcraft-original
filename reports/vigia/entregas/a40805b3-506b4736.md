5. **An edit re-walks only that building.**
   - The program key holds the building's id and its record digest (`entry.sig`), the ground digest, the paving flag, the floor and the tone (cellWork.ts:124).

   - Programs walked by a job that then asks `wider` are dropped again (`programs.undo()`, cellWork.ts:225/:249/:270/:285).
   - Proven by tests/render/cellWork.spec.ts:185, which runs through the real pool:
     - the cell 'mass' job walks every building;
     - the 'massTile' job right after it walks 0 and hits all;
     - its masses are byte-identical to a tile made with the cache cleared;
     - after one building is moved 0.5 m, the cell job walks exactly 1 and hits n−1, and its result equals a recomputation with the cache cleared.

## Technique
- Staged derivation as a shape grammar does it: the mass model first, then the facades (Müller et al. 2006, https://history.siggraph.org/?p=113547).
- Lazy, view-driven derivation with incremental reuse (Steinberger et al. 2014, https://hgpu.org/?p=11204).
- LOD levels as filters of one source, as in UE HLOD (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition---hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine).
- Mass program packed into flat typed arrays: 28.6 KB → 7.9 KB per program, measured by heap on 400 blueprint buildings with a temporary probe that is not committed. This keeps the cache inside the worker heap rule of cellJobMemory.spec (< 4 MB held during a 400-building tile job; it passes).
